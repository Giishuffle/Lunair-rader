# Hiring a customs broker to review the rule library

The single highest-leverage spend on this product. This document is the brief:
what to ask for, what to send them, what "done" looks like, and what it should cost.

## Why we need one

Lunair tells sellers which US import requirements appear to apply to their products.
If that content is wrong, the product is not merely unhelpful - it is harmful, and it
is the fastest way to destroy the trust the whole brand rests on. Nobody on this
project is a customs professional. Software review cannot substitute for domain review.

A licensed reviewer also buys three things beyond accuracy: a defensible answer if a
customer ever challenges our content, a credibility line for marketing
("our requirement library is reviewed by a licensed customs broker"), and a standing
relationship for the questions that will keep arriving.

## Who to look for

- **Licensed Customs Broker (LCB)** - passed the CBP broker exam and holds a federal
  license. This is the credential that matters most.
- **Certified Customs Specialist (CCS)** - NCBFAA certification; good supporting signal.
- Prefer someone who mentions **consulting or advisory work**, e-commerce or Amazon
  sellers, classification services, or compliance audits. Avoid pure freight
  forwarders - they move boxes, which is a different job.
- A solo consultant or boutique is a better fit than a large brokerage: faster, cheaper,
  and actually interested in a small engagement.

## What to ask them to do

Send this as the scope. It is deliberately narrow so it can be quoted quickly.

> **Engagement: review of an import-requirements reference library**
>
> We publish a subscription information service that shows US e-commerce sellers which
> import requirements appear to apply to their products, based on a product description
> they provide. We are not a broker, we file nothing, and we give no advice - every item
> links its official government source and users self-certify their own status.
>
> We would like you to review our requirement library before launch. Specifically:
>
> 1. **Accuracy.** For each product category, are the listed requirements correct,
>    and is anything materially missing?
> 2. **Applicability logic.** We attach requirements to products using simple
>    conditions - product category, whether it contains a battery, whether it is
>    intended for children, country of origin. Are those triggers right, and where
>    are they too broad or too narrow?
> 3. **Wording.** Our descriptions are deliberately plain-English and hedged
>    ("appears to apply", never "you must"). Does any wording overstate certainty or
>    stray toward advice?
> 4. **Classification presentation.** We show two or three candidate HTS codes and ask
>    the user to select and confirm their own. Is that presented responsibly, and how
>    would you word that screen?
> 5. **Blind spots.** What do small importers most commonly get wrong that our
>    library does not currently cover at all?
>
> Deliverable: written comments on the library (a marked-up document or a summary memo
> is fine) plus a one-hour call to walk through the findings. We are happy to pay your
> standard hourly or project rate.

## What to send them

- The rule library, exported as a readable document (one section per category, with
  the plain-English text, the conditions, and the source links).
- A short description of the product and the legal posture - the "Product posture"
  section of `docs/legal/tos-outline.md` is written for exactly this.
- Two or three sample product profiles and the output we would show for each, so they
  can see the end result rather than only the raw rules.

## Scope discipline

Ask for a **fixed-price first review**, not an open retainer. Start with the 5 to 8
categories we intend to launch with rather than all 20 - it keeps the quote low, gets
an answer sooner, and tells us whether this reviewer is a good fit before committing
to more.

## What it should cost

Customs consulting typically bills hourly, with project rates for defined scopes.
A focused review of this size is a small engagement. Budget **$500 to $1,500** for the
first pass on a handful of categories, and expect a higher number if they are asked to
cover all 20 at once. If someone quotes far above that range, the scope has been
misread as ongoing compliance work - re-send the scope above.

## Ongoing

After the first review, the useful shape is a small recurring arrangement: a quarterly
re-review as the library grows, plus an hourly rate for one-off questions. Worth asking
whether they would let us name them as our reviewer in marketing - some will, some
won't, and it is worth more than the review itself if they will.

## Adjacent option worth considering

A licensed broker who likes the product is also a natural first **Lighthouse partner**
(the white-label tier). Ask, at the end of the engagement, whether they would resell
monitoring to their own client book. The review then pays for itself twice.
